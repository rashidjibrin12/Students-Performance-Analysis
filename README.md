## Project Overview

This project analyzes how demographic and socioeconomic factors influence students' performance in math, reading, and writing exams. The goal is to uncover patterns in academic achievement and identify which factors are most strongly associated with success or underperformance.

By examining variables such as gender, race/ethnicity, parental level of education, lunch type, and test preparation course participation, the analysis highlights key drivers of student outcomes. The project combines data cleaning, exploratory analysis, statistical testing, and machine learning to provide both descriptive and predictive insights.

## Business Question

How do factors such as gender, parental education, ethnicity, lunch type, and test preparation impact student performance in exams, and which of these factors are the strongest predictors of academic success?

## Dataset

The project uses the Students Performance in Exams dataset, which contains student records including:
- Gender
- Race/Ethnicity
- Parental Level of Education
- Lunch Type
- Test Preparation Course
- Math Score
- Reading Score
- Writing Score

This dataset was used to analyze both overall student performance and the relationship between demographic/socioeconomic variables and exam results.

## Key Findings

- Female students performed better overall than male students in reading and writing, while math scores were closer across genders.
- Students who completed a test preparation course generally scored higher than those who did not.
- Students with a standard lunch showed stronger performance compared to those with a free/reduced lunch, suggesting a relationship between nutrition and academic outcomes.
- Higher parental education levels were associated with better student performance.
- Ethnicity and demographic background also influenced academic outcomes, indicating that access to support and resources may affect performance.
- Machine learning analysis showed that factors such as parental education, lunch type, and test preparation were among the most influential predictors of academic success.
